Precision Marked Components: Cutting, Edge Quality and Traceability

A small marked component can involve material, cutting, edge quality, code readability and inspection — not just engraving a QR image.

MaterialThin stainless steel

01 • Problem

What needed to be solved

The requirement involved thin stainless-steel components with geometry, holes, identification text and a QR code. A visually present code is not enough if the scanner cannot read it consistently.

01Define the requirement
02Collect evidence
03Review / verify
04Execute & confirm
02 • Evidence

Information that materially changes the answer

  • Material type and thickness
  • Component geometry and edge condition
  • Marking artwork and resolution requirement
  • Readability and contrast requirement
  • Batch quantity
  • Finish and surface preparation
  • Inspection and verification method
03 • Engineering review

How the requirement is broken down

  1. Material evaluation: thickness, finish and laser interaction.
  2. Cutting route: speed, power, gas and nozzle setup for clean edges.
  3. Marking route: artwork resolution, contrast and readable output.
  4. Sequence planning: cutting, finishing, then marking to preserve edge quality.
  5. Inspection: verify all marked components against readability and traceability requirement.
04 • Solution pattern

Practical execution route

A suitable process plan coordinates material selection, laser cutting, edge finishing, permanent marking and traceability. Define and perform the required visual and readability checks before dispatch.

Engineering takeaway

When parts need both cutting and marking, the sequence and edge condition matter as much as the artwork. Poor preparation at the cutting stage makes marking problematic.
