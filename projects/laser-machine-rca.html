Laser Cutting Quality Deterioration: A Structured RCA Path

Poor cutting after optics work can have several causes. The review should separate optics, centering, focus, nozzle, gas, contamination and machine-maintenance evidence.

SymptomCut quality loss
MachineFiber laser
EvidenceOptics + settings
MethodElimination RCA

01 • Problem

What needed to be solved

Cutting quality deteriorated after maintenance/optics intervention. A wide kerf or poor cut can tempt the team to blame the newest replaced component immediately, but multiple interacting variables can produce similar symptoms.

01Define the requirement
02Collect evidence
03Review / verify
04Execute & confirm
02 • Evidence

Information that materially changes the answer

  • Material and thickness
  • Nozzle type / diameter
  • Focus position
  • Lens stack and orientation
  • Protective window condition
  • Nozzle centering result
  • Assist gas and pressure
  • Recent maintenance actions
  • Machine cleanliness
03 • Engineering review

How the requirement is broken down

  1. Verify consumables and optical parts are correct and clean.
  2. Check nozzle condition and centering before changing multiple parameters.
  3. Confirm the focus reference and head calibration.
  4. Compare settings with the previously stable condition.
  5. Record maintenance and contamination evidence to avoid recurring optics damage.
04 • Solution pattern

Practical execution route

The issue is handled as a controlled elimination sequence rather than random parameter changes. Each variable is checked and documented so a confirmed cause can be distinguished from a coincidental recent change.

Engineering takeaway

RCA is not the first plausible story. It is the cause that survives evidence and explains the observed failure mechanism.
