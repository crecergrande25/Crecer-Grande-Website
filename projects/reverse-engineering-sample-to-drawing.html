Engineering guide · Reverse engineering

From a worn sample to a usable drawing.

A damaged part shows what exists now. It does not always show the original size or design intent. This guide explains what to check before making a replacement; it is not a completed customer case study.

Start with the function

Record how the part fits and moves.

Send the sample, photographs in the assembly and any existing drawing. Identify which faces locate the part, which surfaces move and where the damage occurred.

Separate wear from intended geometry

Measure an unworn area where one exists and compare the mating component. A worn bore, damaged thread or broken edge must not be copied as if it were the original specification. Record uncertain dimensions rather than silently guessing them.

Choose datums before dimensions

Identify the mounting face, locating diameter or centerline that controls fit. Measure the critical features from those references so a manufacturer can inspect the replacement against the same basis.

Agree the evidence and the drawing.

What to send

  • Sample condition and the location of wear or failure
  • Assembly photographs and mating-part measurements
  • Known material, treatment and working conditions
  • Quantity and the required inspection or trial-fit checks

What needs approval

  • CAD model and manufacturing drawing
  • Critical dimensions, fits and tolerances
  • Material and finish requirements
  • Any assumptions or dimensions that need a trial fit

Check a first piece before a repeat order.

Where the risk justifies it, agree one inspected first piece and an assembly check before releasing the remaining quantity. Record any approved drawing change so the next batch uses the corrected definition.
