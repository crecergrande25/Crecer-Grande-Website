Holes Near Sheet-Metal Bends: How to Avoid Distortion

A practical design guide for placing holes, slots and hardware features near bends so they do not stretch, ovalize or tear during forming.

Updated 15 Sep 20267 min readPractical reference
Quick answer

Features close to a bend can distort because the material stretches and compresses during forming. A conservative general starting rule is to keep holes and slots several material thicknesses away from the bend zone, then verify against your supplier’s tooling/process rules.

Why holes distort near a bend

During bending, material in and around the bend zone is plastically deformed. If a hole or slot is too close, the deformation can pull the feature oval, shift its center or create tearing around the edge.

Useful design guidance

Protolabs publishes a 4× material-thickness convention for keeping holes/slots away from bend lines in its process guidance, while its detailed toolkit shows that actual minimum distance varies with material, thickness and bend radius. Treat this as process guidance, not a universal standard.

Conservative early-design checkHole-to-bend distance ≳ 4 × material thicknessThen validate using actual bend radius, material and supplier tooling.

When a feature must be close

  • Add a relief cut around the feature.
  • Move the hole to a secondary operation after bending.
  • Change bend radius or flange geometry.
  • Use hardware placement rules from the insert manufacturer.
  • Agree an acceptable distortion/tolerance with manufacturing.

Hardware inserts

Self-clinching nuts/studs require adequate distance from both edges and formed features. Use the specific hardware manufacturer’s minimum centerline-to-edge and installation-thickness data.

Drawing communication

Dimension critical hole locations from functional datums on the formed part where assembly matters. If a hole is drilled after forming, make that process intent clear in the manufacturing route.

Engineering note

This article is general guidance for preliminary engineering and shop-floor understanding. The drawing, applicable standard, machine/tooling OEM instructions, process-specific safety requirements and qualified engineering judgment take priority.
