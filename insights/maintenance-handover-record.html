Maintenance Handover: What Must Be Recorded

A maintenance job is not complete when the tools are removed. The receiving team needs to know what was found, what changed, what was verified and which restrictions or follow-up actions remain.

All 50 insights →
InsightsMaintenance

An incomplete handover can allow a temporary repair or unverified adjustment to become the assumed normal state. Clear records preserve the distinction between restored function and confirmed root-cause correction.

Practical checksCommon mistakesWhat to collectThe next decision

Practical checks

  • Identify the equipment, work order and isolated systems involved.
  • Record replaced parts, adjustments and test results.
  • List unresolved issues, restrictions and the person responsible for each action.
  • Use the site procedure for restoring guards, clearing isolation and authorizing service.

Record the finding against the actual part, machine or drawing revision. A useful check identifies both the observation and the conditions under which it was made, so another person can review it.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not treat a successful brief run as proof of long-term reliability.
  • Do not remove another person’s isolation or bypass the site release procedure.

What to collect before a review

  • Job scope and findings
  • Parts and adjustment records
  • Verification results and outstanding actions

Send clear original files where available. Mark uncertain dimensions, missing information and the result you need. Separate confirmed requirements from assumptions instead of filling the gaps with guessed specifications.

The next decision

Have the responsible personnel accept the handover and service status. Any temporary measure should have explicit limits and a follow-up plan rather than an informal verbal agreement.
